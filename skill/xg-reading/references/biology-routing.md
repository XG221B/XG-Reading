# Biology routing

Choose one primary module based on the decisive evidence, not merely the journal category. Add one secondary module only when necessary.

## Molecular and cellular mechanism

Use `molecular-cellular.md` when central claims depend on gene/protein perturbation, localization, signaling, organelles, cell state, biochemical pathways, or causal mechanism.

## Omics and computational biology

Use `omics-computational.md` when central claims depend on bulk or single-cell sequencing, spatial omics, proteomics, metabolomics, screening, computational prediction, clustering, integration, or model-derived biological inference.

## Immunology and microbiology

Use `immunology-microbiology.md` when central claims depend on immune populations, activation/exhaustion, host-pathogen interactions, microbial communities, infection burden, virulence, vaccines, or immune intervention.

## Structural biology and methods

Use `structural-methods.md` when central claims depend on cryo-EM, crystallography, NMR, molecular interfaces, engineered assays, imaging tools, measurement platforms, or performance benchmarking.

## Translational biology and bioengineering

Use `translational-engineering.md` when central claims depend on biomaterials, drug delivery, nanomedicine, devices, animal therapy, pharmacology, biodistribution, safety, patient samples, cohorts, or clinical endpoints.

## Hybrid studies

Select the module that governs the strongest mechanistic or outcome claim as primary. Add one secondary module only for a distinct evidence layer. Examples:

- single-cell discovery followed by CRISPR validation: omics primary, molecular secondary;
- biomaterial therapy with immune mechanism: translational primary, immunology secondary;
- cryo-EM interface plus cellular signaling: structural primary, molecular secondary.

Do not read all modules “for completeness”. Depth comes from applying the right module rigorously.

## Review and perspective papers

Do not pretend conceptual figures are primary data. Reconstruct the review's thesis, evidence selection, competing models, source coverage, unresolved controversies, and omitted counterevidence. Use the domain module matching the review's subject, but replace panel-level experimental claims with source-level evidence mapping.
