# Translational biology and bioengineering

## Materials, delivery, and devices

- Separate fabrication, physicochemical characterization, stability, functional performance, biological interaction, efficacy, and safety.
- Verify loading, release, recovery, batch consistency, storage, sterilization, and function after formulation.
- Distinguish active transport or targeting from enhanced diffusion, retention, endpoint accumulation, or altered clearance.
- Require controls that isolate carrier, cargo, targeting ligand, propulsion or stimulus, and combination effects when claims depend on modular synergy.

## Pharmacology and in vivo studies

- Check dose, route, schedule, exposure, biodistribution, pharmacokinetics, tissue penetration, clearance, and relevant comparator.
- Identify the experimental unit, randomization, blinding, exclusions, sex, age, model limitations, humane endpoints, and longitudinal attrition.
- Distinguish surrogate markers from functional disease endpoints.
- Separate local efficacy from systemic exposure and acute tolerability from chronic safety.
- Check whether efficacy depends on a mechanism using depletion, blockade, rescue, pharmacodynamic markers, or tissue-level validation.

## Human samples and clinical studies

- Determine design: retrospective, prospective, case-control, cohort, diagnostic, interventional, or randomized trial.
- Check eligibility, endpoint definition, missing data, confounding, multiplicity, model calibration, subgroup credibility, and external validation.
- Distinguish association, prognosis, prediction, treatment effect, and clinical utility.
- Animal or ex vivo evidence does not establish human efficacy; patient-sample association does not establish therapeutic benefit.

## Translation boundaries

Assess manufacturability, quality control, reproducibility, immune response, long-term safety, regulatory fit, workflow burden, and realistic clinical alternatives. Do not demand every translation experiment from an early mechanistic paper, but keep the conclusion within its stage.
