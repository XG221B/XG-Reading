# Structural biology and methods

## Structural evidence

- Check resolution, local resolution, map/model agreement, validation metrics, conformational heterogeneity, particle or crystal selection, and missing regions.
- Distinguish density-supported geometry from modeled or flexible regions.
- A spatially plausible contact is not automatically a functional interface.
- Test interface claims with targeted mutants while checking folding, expression, localization, and nonspecific loss of function.
- Separate static structure, conformational inference, biochemical kinetics, and cellular function.

## Biochemical and biophysical assays

- Inspect purity, stoichiometry, concentration range, binding model, kinetic regime, controls, and replicate structure.
- Distinguish affinity from occupancy, catalytic rate, specificity, and cellular relevance.
- Check whether tags, immobilization, buffer, or truncation could alter behavior.

## Method and tool papers

- Identify the claimed advantage: sensitivity, specificity, resolution, throughput, speed, robustness, cost, or accessibility.
- Require fair baselines, matched inputs, transparent parameter tuning, and representative as well as difficult cases.
- Inspect dynamic range, detection limit, false positives, false negatives, reproducibility, and failure modes.
- Separate proof-of-principle from broad applicability.
- Check performance on independent laboratories, specimens, instruments, batches, or real-world samples where the claim requires it.

## Common overclaims

- functional mechanism inferred from structure alone;
- direct interaction inferred from proximity;
- broad superiority inferred from one favorable benchmark;
- robustness inferred without perturbing acquisition or analysis conditions.
