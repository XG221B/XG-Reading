# Immunology and microbiology

## Immune-cell evidence

- Read the full gating strategy, parent population, viability and singlet gates, compensation, fluorescence-minus-one controls, and absolute counts where relevant.
- Distinguish abundance, activation, differentiation, exhaustion, trafficking, and effector function.
- Marker expression alone rarely establishes function; seek cytokine production, killing, proliferation, suppression, transfer, depletion, blockade, or challenge experiments.
- Check whether tissue digestion, sampling time, or treatment changes cell recovery.

## Mechanistic immune claims

- Depletion or knockout supports necessity only if depletion efficiency and off-target effects are shown.
- Adoptive transfer supports sufficiency only within the transfer context.
- Cytokine changes require cellular source and functional relevance, not just bulk concentration.
- Distinguish local and systemic responses and acute activation from durable memory.

## Microbiology and infection

- Separate colonization, burden, viability, virulence, transmission, and host damage.
- Check inoculum, multiplicity of infection, growth phase, strain background, culture conditions, and antibiotic selection.
- For microbiome studies, inspect extraction controls, contamination, compositionality, sequencing depth, cage/batch effects, and host covariates.
- Association between taxa and phenotype does not establish microbial causality; seek isolation, defined consortia, transfer, depletion, complementation, or metabolite validation.
- For antimicrobial claims, distinguish growth inhibition, killing, persistence, resistance evolution, and host-mediated effects.

## Common overclaims

- immune activation inferred from one cytokine or marker;
- antitumor immunity inferred without functional or depletion evidence;
- pathogen clearance inferred from signal loss without viability measurement;
- microbiome causality inferred from relative-abundance correlation.
